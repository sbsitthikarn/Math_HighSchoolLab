    Key ideas from Graphs of Quadratic Functions. Review them before you practise.

    General form

    y = ax2 + bx + c, a ≠ 0

    • a > 0 opens upwards with a minimum point · a < 0 opens downwards with a maximum point
    • The closer a is to 0, the wider the graph
    • The graph cuts the y-axis at exactly one point, (0, c)
    • The line of symmetry x = −b ÷ 2a passes through the turning point
    Completed-square form

    y = a(x − h)2 + k

    • The turning point is (h, k) and the line of symmetry is x = h
    • Watch the sign: (x + 3)2 means h = −3
    • Completing the square: x2 + bx = (x + b/2)2 − (b/2)2
    Factorised form

    y = ±(x − p)(x − q)

    • The graph cuts the x-axis at x = p and x = q
    • The line of symmetry is halfway between: x = (p + q) ÷ 2
    • The y-intercept is pq (or −pq when the bracket has a minus sign in front)
    Graphical method

    Solving quadratic equations graphically

    • The roots of ax2 + bx + c = 0 are the x-coordinates where the graph cuts the x-axis
    • Cuts twice → 2 distinct roots · touches once → repeated roots · never meets → no real roots
    • To solve f(x) = k, draw the line y = k and read the x-coordinates of the intersections
    • To solve f(x) = mx + d, draw the line y = mx + d on the same axes
    • Rearrange so one side matches the graph you have; the other side is the line to draw
    Applications

    Applied problems

    • The maximum or minimum of a quantity is the y-coordinate of the turning point, reached when x (or t) equals its x-coordinate
    • The value at t = 0 is the starting value, such as the height of a building
    • When an object hits the ground, h = 0; keep only answers that make sense (t ≥ 0)
    • Use symmetry: points at the same height are the same distance from the line of symmetry
